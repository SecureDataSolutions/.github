# SDS service and publishing standard

Keep service definitions brief, evidence-based and reusable. Help the team ship useful work while the wider site and catalog evolve.

## Six answers for a new or materially changed offer

1. **Customer and problem** — Who buys it, what is happening, and why does it matter?
2. **Value and result** — What useful decision, capability or improvement does the customer receive?
3. **Family and boundary** — Choose Security & Defensive Engineering, AI & Workflow Automation, or Networks & Infrastructure. State scope, prerequisites, deliverables, implementation and verification. Add a secondary family when useful. MSP delivery is a delivery model. A new family needs Ed's decision before publication.
4. **Evidence and delivery** — What can SDS deliver today, what supports each material claim, what is proposed, and who delivers it?
5. **Language and discovery** — Use approved company wording and familiar buyer questions. State the intended page and relationship to existing services.
6. **Commercial approval** — Reference approved prices or quoted-by-scope terms, included work and Ed's approval for new commitments. Do not invent pricing or guarantees.

Reuse approved answers by reference. A typo or README branding change does not need a fresh service definition.

## Approved company copy

**Security, AI & Systems Engineering**

Secure Data Solutions helps businesses strengthen their security and put AI to work. We engineer the systems and workflows that reduce repetitive work, help teams understand and respond to threats, and turn security findings into verified improvements.

Keep familiar services such as firewall engineering, switching, routing and penetration testing discoverable where relevant to the actual offering. The company description spans security, AI and systems; tool-specific descriptions explain the particular work. Founded in 2007 is supporting company history, not a claim of AI delivery since 2007.

## Four short answers in a public-content PR

- **Customer and source:** What changes for the reader? Link the approved definition or company copy.
- **SEO and AI search:** Which buyer question is served? State changes to URLs, titles, descriptions, visible answers, internal links and crawl/index behavior, as applicable. Preserve useful entry terms. A no-impact answer needs a reason. Expected improvements are hypotheses, not ranking promises.
- **Claims and commitments:** Identify changed claims, scope, prices or promises and their evidence/approval. State when unchanged.
- **Verification and follow-up:** Record relevant build, rendered-content and link checks. Record how material search changes will be monitored and queue optional refinements.

For website URL changes, address redirects, canonical URLs and sitemaps. Keep structured data consistent with visible content. Check crawler access when affected.

## Finish the useful release

- Start from the nearest authorized customer or publishing outcome and a small definition of done.
- Reuse settled decisions. Do not require a complete rebrand, catalog or perfect site before publishing a truthful tool, README or service page.
- Missing information blocks only the dependent claim. Optional polish goes in the backlog.
- Stop for a material inaccuracy, unsafe behavior, a broken customer journey, an unapproved commitment or a real permission requirement.
- When relevant checks pass and authorization is present, finish and report the actual result.

## Task ownership and acceptance

Use the existing project task identifier, including an SD alias when assigned. Before editing, record owner, branch/check-out, file scope and completion condition. One writer owns each file. The implementer records the commit, checks and remaining limitations; a separate reviewer records acceptance when independent review is required. Never claim Accepted or published from an implementation-only receipt.

## Enforcement boundary

These are contributor instructions. A README, template, AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md is not a GitHub merge gate. Required PR checks and branch rules must be installed and demonstrated separately in each repository. Human review still judges the claims; a form checker cannot prove good SEO or business value.
